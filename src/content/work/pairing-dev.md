## Challenge

Pairing.dev runs several B2B SaaS products in parallel, StatsAware, CribCRM, and more, on the theory that a portfolio of recurring-revenue products beats betting everything on one. When I joined, the founder was still handling marketing himself. There was no marketing function, lead generation was immature, and most products earned little or no recurring revenue. They needed one person to own growth across every product at once.

## Approach

I started with the outbound engine because pipeline was the bottleneck. I built an automated system that sources and qualifies leads before a single email goes out, then feeds them into segmented cold sequences with copy written and tested per buyer. The automation is the multiplier: n8n and LLM workflows (Claude, ChatGPT) handle list building, research, personalization, and campaign assembly, which cut execution time by roughly 70% and let one marketer run what normally needs a team.

Then the role widened past acquisition. I took on product marketing and positioning for StatsAware and CribCRM, built and optimized landing pages (90+ variations across client campaigns), ran a content pipeline of three YouTube videos a week plus short-form, wrote onboarding and sales-enablement material, and supported QA on the products themselves. I work directly with the founder on go-to-market and analytics the way an in-house growth lead would.

## Result

- The SaaS portfolio now runs at roughly **$22K in monthly recurring revenue**, up from little or none when I joined
- Landed a **€10,000 upfront enterprise deal** that brought in 30+ sub-clients, one of the studio's largest commercial wins
- Built an automated pipeline that sources and auto-qualifies **1,500+ leads a week**, and ran cold-email sequences at a **23% open and 6% reply rate**
- **CribCRM** became the studio's strongest-performing product, with growth, onboarding, and launch marketing I owned end to end
- Cut campaign execution time **~70%** with AI workflows I built in-house

*Fully remote across four time zones since day one, async by default.*
